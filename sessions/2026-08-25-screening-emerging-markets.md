### ANTA Sports Products (2020.HK)

**Open item from 08-04 resolved:** the FY2025 net-income "decline" (13,588 CNY mn vs FY2024's 15,596 CNY mn) was a **comparison-base artifact**, not margin compression. FY2024's headline figure included a non-cash "deemed disposal" accounting gain from Amer Sports' NYSE listing/share placing (an investee issuing new shares dilutes and revalues the parent's stake under HKFRS with no cash changing hands). Excluding that one-off, FY2024 core profit was RMB 11,927mn — so FY2025's 13,588 CNY mn is actually **+13.9% growth** against the correct base, matching ANTA's own reported figure. Operating profit rose 15.0% YoY (op. margin +0.4pp to 23.8%); the reported 62.0% gross margin (−0.2pp) reflects a genuine mix-shift toward technical/premium product and e-commerce, not integration costs or Olympics spend (Amer Sports' profit contribution to ANTA actually improved YoY).

### Proya Cosmetics (603605.SH) — new candidate, first qualitative pass

1. **Why are margins high?** Gross margin has actually been rising (73.26% FY2025, +1.87pp YoY) even as revenue weakened — genuine mid-price-tier formulation/brand pricing power, not scale. But the sales-expense ratio climbed to 49.63% of revenue (from 46.78%) — gross-margin gains are being consumed by rising customer-acquisition cost on Tmall/Douyin, which is why net margin (14.13%) hasn't kept pace. 2. **What would it take to compete?** Moderate moat, not deep. Proya built in-house livestreaming capability since 2021 to reduce dependence on paid influencers — real but replicable distribution investment. No sourced evidence of a buyback program either way. This sits in direct tension with the 18.40% 3yr revenue CAGR that cleared the quant gate — that trailing figure is carried by strong 2022–2024 growth and is now stale relative to the two most recent reported periods, where the trend has already inflected negative. Compounding: rising customer-acquisition cost on a shrinking base; intensifying domestic competition (KANS outgrowing Proya sharply); ~98.7% revenue concentration in mainland China; rising regulatory compliance cost under China's CSAR cosmetics-safety framework (phased in 2025–2026, $15K–150K per-SKU compliance cost depending on timing) that disproportionately burdens a fast-launch, multi-brand portfolio; and capital being redirected toward unproven international expansion/M&A at the exact moment the core engine is decelerating.

FY2025 was Proya's first-ever down year and Q1 2026 confirmed the decline is continuing — a trend inflection the trailing 3yr CAGR entirely masks. The moat rests more on distribution/content execution within platforms Proya doesn't control than on durable brand loyalty, consistent with the sourced disruption evidence (AI-driven livestreaming and content-format shifts already reallocating share mid-cycle). Capital allocation (bolt-on M&A, dividend, HK listing) looks disciplined, and the multi-brand diversification is a legitimate response, but international revenue is still ~1.3% of total — no proven second growth engine yet. **Recommendation: if `/new-position Proya` is run, the deceleration must be explicitly weighed rather than the name being waved through on its trailing quant numbers alone; a rescore trigger should watch closely for whether FY2026 shows stabilization or further flagship-brand erosion.**

## Glossary

- **CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate)** — the smoothed annual growth rate that would take a starting value to an ending value over N years, accounting for compounding.
- **ROE (Return on Equity)** — net income as a percentage of shareholders' equity; a profitability/efficiency measure.
- **ROIC (Return on Invested Capital)** — net operating profit after tax divided by total invested capital (debt + equity); a cleaner measure than ROE for companies with unusual capital structures.
- **ROCE (Return on Capital Employed)** — a close cousin of ROIC, used here as a proxy where a source doesn't publish ROIC directly.
- **FCF (Free Cash Flow)** — operating cash flow minus capital expenditure; cash actually available to the business after maintaining/growing operations.
- **FCF Yield** — FCF divided by market cap (or enterprise value); how much free cash the business throws off relative to what you're paying for it.
- **EV/EBIT (Enterprise Value / Earnings Before Interest & Tax)** — a capital-structure-neutral valuation multiple; lower means cheaper relative to operating earnings.
- **Net Debt/EBITDA** — a leverage ratio; negative means the company holds more cash than debt (net cash).
- **Gross margin** — (revenue − cost of goods sold) ÷ revenue; a measure of pricing power/production efficiency before operating costs.
- **Net margin** — net income ÷ revenue; the percentage of each dollar of sales that becomes profit after all costs.
- **TTM (Trailing Twelve Months)** — the most recent 12 months of reported financial data, used as a rolling "current" snapshot between annual reports.
- **YoY (Year-over-Year)** — comparing a period to the same period one year earlier.
- **GAAP / Non-GAAP** — GAAP is standard accounting-rule reported figures; non-GAAP ("adjusted") strips out certain items (e.g. stock-based compensation) to show management's view of core operating performance.
- **MAU / DAU (Monthly / Daily Active Users)** — count of unique users engaging with a platform in that window, a scale/engagement metric for internet businesses.
- **De minimis** — a customs threshold below which imported parcels enter duty-free; the EU reduced this exemption effective July 1, 2026, directly raising the landed cost of low-value China-origin parcels (core to Temu's shipping model).
- **ADR (American Depositary Receipt)** — a US-listed security representing shares of a foreign company, letting US investors trade it without a foreign brokerage account.
- **Moat** — a durable competitive advantage that protects a business's profits from competitors over time (network effects, brand, switching costs, scale, etc.).
- **DTC (Direct-to-Consumer)** — a distribution model where a company sells directly to end customers rather than through third-party wholesalers/retailers, giving it pricing control and first-party data.
- **Deemed disposal gain / equity dilution gain** — a non-cash accounting gain recognized when a company's stake in an investee is diluted by that investee issuing new shares to outside investors (e.g. in an IPO) at a price above book value; no cash is received, but the retained stake is revalued upward through profit and loss.
- **Structural triage** — Step 1 of the `/screen` process: eliminating obviously-disqualified candidates on business-model grounds before spending analysis effort on detailed financials.
- **Data gap** — a required metric that could not be sourced (or, here, a sourced figure with an internal-consistency problem) and, per CLAUDE.md Rule 0, is flagged rather than estimated or silently substituted.
